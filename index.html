How the peg pyramid works

The board is arranged as a triangle of staggered pegs. Each row is slightly wider than the one above it, so a ball hitting the top has a fifty-fifty chance of drifting left or right. That same split repeats at every row until the ball reaches the bottom and lands in one of the pockets.

Because every bounce acts like a coin flip, most balls settle near the centre while only a few reach the outer edges. Mathematicians call this pattern a binomial distribution, and it explains why the middle pockets appear often while edge pockets pay far more.
